The Hype of Pre-Orders

How a risky Galaxy S10 launch experiment evolved into a repeatable, multi-channel pre-order system spanning paid media, creative operations, content, partner channels and landing pages.

StrategySearch & ShoppingCreativeContent & VideoPartner channelsLanding pagesResults
1 · Strategy

From “Beyond” to a launch operating system

One month after joining the company in early 2019, I prioritized a set of experiments around the upcoming Samsung flagship internally known as Beyond—the Galaxy S10. The idea was risky: our monthly budget was materially smaller than major Ukrainian retailers, so the strategy could not rely on spend alone.

The answer was a highly active launch process: close monitoring of budgets, auction position, competitor offers, landing pages, feed quality and creative performance during the roughly two-week pre-order window and the first week of active sales.

The objective was not to outspend larger competitors. It was to react faster, improve the offer continuously and use every channel as part of one launch system.
2 · Paid acquisition

Search and Shopping under launch pressure

During launch days, top competitors were constantly adjusting ads and offers. I would replace weaker-converting ads multiple times per day and change destination pages as the pre-order and sales phases evolved. Budgets and conversion efficiency required continuous attention because acquisition costs could escalate quickly.

Shopping required a different kind of discipline: checking whether new product descriptions, images and URLs were correctly pulled into the feed, passed moderation and remained competitive. When something failed, manual fixes had to happen fast.

~20 checks/day

During critical periods, competitor offers, ad copy, positions, spend signals and landing-page quality could be reviewed repeatedly throughout the day.

3 · Creative operations

Banner systems built for the first 3–5 days

Display and Discovery became a major experiment. In the active phase I checked whether every required format passed moderation, gained initial delivery, and moved toward expected reach, clicks and conversions. Most banner performance tended to concentrate in the first few days, which made launch timing critical.

For one campaign design, assets could span website formats (1590×430, 776×300, 400×400), social (800×600, 1080×1080), large-format displays (3840×2160, 1080×1920), and a full Google Display / Discovery set including 1200×628, 1200×1200 and standard display sizes.

~300 assets

Across different designs during an intensive 21-day pre-order and launch cycle, compared with a previous ceiling of about 50 banners per month.

4 · Content & video

Native content, SEO and video as conversion support

Native and SEO-oriented articles became a regular tool for extending brand reach and adding conversion opportunities. Over time, the content production process became more systematic. Video followed a similar trajectory: reviews required additional scripting, YouTube descriptions and promotion, but they helped grow the channel and support incremental sales. Short-form content was also used to reach younger audiences.

5 · Partner channels

Telegram, push, price aggregators and email

Not every channel deserved equal priority. Push notifications had low conversion with a subscriber base below roughly 3,000, so resources were focused on stronger channels. Telegram articles on high-interest launches could support both sales and SEO. Price aggregators were especially useful when campaign naming, promotional copy, click pricing and new platform features were handled quickly and strategically.

Email remained secondary initially, with campaigns through partners such as Hotline, Kyivstar, Justin, AIN and internal lists. By early 2021 it became more interesting as Viber costs increased and the site matured enough to support automated lifecycle ideas such as reminders after incomplete conversion actions.

6 · Landing pages

Nearly 1,000 pages developed with my involvement

Across roughly two years, the team developed around 1,000 web pages with my participation. Pre-order landings, launch pages, follow-up sales pages and native content all played a role in retaining attention and converting traffic. Under launch deadlines, the challenge was to make these pages unique and useful despite limited time and resources.

7 · Outcome

From two flagship pre-orders a year to nine product launches

Before my arrival, pre-orders were mainly run twice a year for S- and Note-series flagships. After the Galaxy S10/S10+ pre-order program, the number of products receiving pre-order treatment expanded from roughly two to nine per year, including mid-range smartphones, experimental foldables, premium TVs, wearables, tablets, headphones and SmartTags.

The archived case notes state that pre-order revenue for the S10/S10+ launch was about twice the previous 2018 pre-order revenue, and that the company ranked among Samsung’s top five partners in Europe/CIS for those pre-order sales.

Working principle: critical launch issues across search, Shopping, banners, Discovery, YouTube, local Google campaigns and social were handled immediately whenever possible. The goal was to avoid waiting for the next working day when a temporary solution could protect performance.
